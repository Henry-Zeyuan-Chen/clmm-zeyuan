# Member C: shared training and evaluation

Zeyuan is responsible for pretraining feasibility, reusable baseline weights, and a common evaluation contract. Member A owns sleep consolidation, and Member B owns the selective Hebbian/neural-memory modification. The team's final proposal asks whether those interventions improve learning new associations, retaining unchanged facts and handling real revisions. Sleep timing must be compared with continuous replay at the same replay budget. The Hebbian track must distinguish the update rule from selective routing. Both need adequate acquisition before a retention gain can be interpreted.

## Current evidence and its limits

The [shared byte-language pilot](SHARED_PRETRAIN.md) supplies two complete model checkpoints and a modest language-context memory benefit. The [fact/revision evaluation](FACT_REVISION.md) supplies causal matched replay schedules and exposes failed acquisition. The [paired-query diagnostic](PAIRED_ACQUISITION.md) supplies stronger single-fact recall and revision checkpoints, but fails two-key selection even when direct attention sees the facts. These are compact lucidrains-based engineering pilots, not paper-scale pretrained Titans or a demonstrated sleep/Hebbian result.

The [fixed-set fitting and assignment-transfer diagnostic](FIXED_SET_GENERALIZATION.md) is complete, including its declared fresh-assignment follow-up. All four fixed-set models fit 32/32 contexts; none learns reliable transfer. The follow-up scores only 1/128 and 3/128 both-correct on training-disjoint novel assignments. It establishes finite-set trainability and a transfer failure under these recipes, without identifying an architecture impossibility. Training-set fitting, novel assignments on familiar keys, unseen keys and unseen wording remain separate measured abilities. Checkpoint qualifications are recorded explicitly; none is promoted to a retention baseline.

## Deliverables for the method tracks

1. **Weights and provenance.** Keep both original parents and every declared adaptation checkpoint with architecture/configuration, source/data identity and training budget. Mark each checkpoint's demonstrated capabilities and unresolved failures; do not silently promote a good fitting score into a retention baseline. Replacement memory modules need an explicit compatible adapter, not arbitrary weight loading.
2. **Common evaluation.** Preserve disjoint assignment streams, label-independent queries, frozen outer weights, state reset, acquisition/revision/unchanged-fact outcomes, buffer size and matched replay/update opportunities. Report context-visible controls and distinguish stale conflicting replay from forgetting. Fixed-prefix recomputation is the current diagnostic interface; persistent cross-session write/read/state behavior remains to be integrated and tested.
3. **Training feasibility and stronger baseline.** Keep byte-pilot costs distinct from the paper's subword/FineWeb-Edu-scale setting. A future tokenizer must be saved and trained only on training data, with compatible newly trained embeddings/output weights. Measure actual throughput and context coverage before choosing a corpus/model budget. Further architecture or curriculum changes must follow the fitting/transfer evidence, with finite search budgets.

## Admission gates and next project milestone

A useful common retention baseline must learn more than one association, answer the requested key under fresh assignments, and handle genuine revisions. Then evaluate stable versus superseded facts at increasing delays, including targets evicted from the replay buffer. For the memory-dependent setting, verify useful cross-segment/state information and causality; for persistent sessions, verify write/read/reset semantics and frozen outer parameters. Compare method variants at similar acquisition, with matched declared training and inference resources.

A small closed-vocabulary success can be a labeled mechanism test, while unseen-key or language-template transfer remains unresolved. It must not replace a failed broad-generalization result. The immediate next shared milestone is an acquisition-qualified checkpoint plus this evaluation interface for both teammate modules. Broader language pretraining and cross-session retention are subsequent Member C work; a class report should summarize observed progress and limits rather than claim those milestones prematurely.

After the fitting/transfer diagnosis, prioritize a controlled common key/value representation as a positive control for the memory mechanisms, and a bounded stronger-encoder/association-training comparison for the complete sequence model. Keep the byte model as a documented fitting/generalization control until it passes the relevant acquisition checks. This lets the two method tracks progress without interpreting weak baseline recall as a sleep/Hebbian finding.
